What this shows — and what it does not. The points collapse onto operator-level rows. A campus's PUE is usually its operator's fleet figure, and its water figure is the operator's fleet WUE applied to site IT load. Neither is a meter reading. That clustering is the finding: at this level of disclosure, site efficiency is operator efficiency. The matrix is the part that is genuinely per-site. A liquid-cooled rack can still sit behind an evaporative plant, and an air-cooled hall can run on dry coolers.

05 / HOW TO READ THE NUMBERS

Three metrics.
Three different questions.

Scale, facility efficiency, and water intensity are related—but none can stand in for the others.

01

How much compute?

H100e = compute ÷ 1.979 PFLOPS

H100-equivalents normalize announced GPU counts, third-party estimates, peak FLOPS, or power. The most direct available signal wins.

How much overhead?

PUE = facility energy ÷ IT energy

A PUE of 1.10 means 10 units of facility overhead for every 100 units of IT energy. It says nothing about GPU utilization or work per joule.

How much water?

WUE = water litres ÷ IT kWh

WUE can describe withdrawal or consumption. Those are not interchangeable, and a single efficient site is not a fleet-wide result.

WATER · CHECK THE BASIS
i

The honest efficiency metric is still missing. A comparable answer would fix the reporting year, facility boundary, and utilization—then measure useful work such as tokens or FLOPS per joule.
